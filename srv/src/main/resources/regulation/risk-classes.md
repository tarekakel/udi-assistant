# Risk classification (EU MDR 2017/745, Article 51 and Annex VIII)

## The four classes
Devices are divided into classes I, IIa, IIb and III according to the intended purpose and the inherent risks. Classification is determined by the rules in Annex VIII, which consider duration of contact (transient, short term, long term), degree of invasiveness, whether the device is active, and the part of the body affected.

## Typical examples
Class I: non-invasive devices such as examination gloves, wheelchairs, simple wound dressings that act as a mechanical barrier. Class IIa: devices such as hearing aids, most hydrogel and interactive wound dressings, short-term surgically invasive instruments. Class IIb: long-term implants such as bone screws and plates, ventilators, infusion pumps. Class III: devices in direct contact with the heart or central nervous system, long-term implants with a biological effect, catheters for intracranial pressure monitoring.

## Conformity assessment by class
Class I devices are self-certified by the manufacturer, except class I sterile (Is), measuring (Im) and reusable surgical (Ir) devices, which require notified body involvement for the relevant aspects. Class IIa, IIb and III devices require a notified body. Class III and implantable devices are subject to the strictest assessment, including a clinical evaluation consultation procedure for certain class III devices.

## Class changes and the UDI record
Reclassification of a device changes the Basic UDI-DI grouping because risk class is one of the attributes shared by devices under one Basic UDI-DI. A change in risk class therefore requires a review of the Basic UDI-DI and of the certificates that reference it.

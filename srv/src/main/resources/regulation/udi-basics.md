# UDI basics (EU MDR 2017/745, Article 27 and Annex VI)

## What a UDI is
The Unique Device Identification (UDI) system is a series of numeric or alphanumeric characters created through internationally accepted device identification and coding standards. It allows the unambiguous identification of specific devices on the market and supports traceability, incident reporting, field safety corrective actions and the fight against falsified devices. The UDI consists of two parts: the UDI-DI and the UDI-PI.

## UDI-DI versus UDI-PI
The UDI-DI (Device Identifier) is a unique numeric or alphanumeric code specific to a model of device. It is static: it identifies the manufacturer and the device model and is the access key to information stored in the UDI database. The UDI-PI (Production Identifier) is dynamic: it identifies the unit of device production, for example the serial number, lot or batch number, software identification, or manufacturing or expiry date. The UDI-DI is registered in the database; the UDI-PI is not stored in EUDAMED but appears on the label.

## Basic UDI-DI
The Basic UDI-DI is the primary identifier of a device model. It is assigned at the level of the device unit of use and is the main key for records in the UDI database. It groups devices with the same intended purpose, risk class and essential design and manufacturing characteristics. The Basic UDI-DI does not appear on the label or packaging; it is referenced in the EU declaration of conformity, in the technical documentation and in certificates issued by notified bodies.

## Issuing entities
UDIs must be issued by an entity designated by the European Commission. The designated issuing entities are GS1, HIBCC (Health Industry Business Communications Council), ICCBBA and IFA GmbH. A GS1 UDI-DI is a GTIN, most commonly presented as a 14-digit GTIN-14 whose last digit is a check digit calculated with the GS1 modulo-10 algorithm.
